MINIMAL-PERIOD CYCLIC PACKING OF MULTI-CHANNEL FOOTPRINTS.

I am studying a scheduling object and I need the structural boundary between the tractable and
the intractable, plus a dual certificate. Please answer as a mathematician: definitions, theorem
statements, proofs or proof sketches, explicit counterexamples. At most 2500 words.

============================================================
1. THE OBJECT
============================================================

Let R be a finite set of "channels". Each channel r carries a capacity m_r, a positive integer:
at each integer instant, channel r admits at most m_r simultaneous occupancies.

A "cell" is a pair (r, t) with r in R and t an integer instant.

Let V be a finite set of atoms. Each atom i carries a FOOTPRINT F_i, a finite subset of
R x {0,1,2,...}. Placing atom i at instant s_i means it occupies exactly the cells
    { (r, s_i + d) : (r,d) in F_i }.
A placement is admissible when every cell (r,t) is occupied by at most m_r atoms.

Precedence: a directed graph D on V. An arc i -> j carries an integer length l_ij >= 0 and an
integer turn count w_ij >= 0.

Periodic placement of period T: every atom i is placed at all instants s_i + kT, k in Z, and the
precedence constraint reads
    s_j - s_i >= l_ij - T * w_ij.
Occupancy of channel r at residue class t mod T counts all atoms whose footprint hits it in any
repetition. Objective: minimise T over admissible periodic placements. Call the optimum T*.

Two classical lower bounds:
  (B1) volume, per channel:   T* >= ceil( ( sum over i of |{d : (r,d) in F_i}| ) / m_r ).
  (B2) circuits:              T* >= max over directed circuits C of ( sum l ) / ( sum w ).

============================================================
2. WHAT MAKES THIS OBJECT DIFFERENT FROM THE TEXTBOOK ONE
============================================================

In the textbook version each atom occupies ONE cell of ONE channel for a contiguous span, and
then (B1),(B2) and greedy behave well. Here an atom occupies a SET of cells spread over SEVERAL
channels at SEVERAL offsets, and the sets of different atoms differ in shape. Empirically the
shapes we meet are of three kinds:

  (S1) singletons: F_i = {(r,0)} -- one cell in one channel.
  (S2) a single cell in a "dispatch" channel r0 at offset 0, PLUS a contiguous run of tau cells
       in a "heavy" channel r1 at offsets 0..tau-1. Measured tau = 2, m_{r0} = 1, m_{r1} = 1.
  (S3) a single cell in r0 at offset 0, PLUS k cells in a "transfer" channel r2 at offsets
       0..k-1, where k depends on the atom (k in {1,2,4,8}) and m_{r2} counts a divisible
       capacity (a width, 128 units per instant, an atom consuming between 4 and 128 units).

So: one shared unit-capacity channel touched by EVERY atom at offset 0, plus per-atom runs in
other channels. Genuine multi-channel footprints, but of a very restricted form.

============================================================
3. THE EMPIRICAL FACTS THE MODEL MUST REPRODUCE
============================================================

These are measurements on the physical realisation, all with the answer held bit-identical, and
each has already refuted at least one of my earlier models. Any theory I adopt must reproduce
them; I list them so you can test candidate structures against them.

(E1) FRACTIONAL AND PLACE-DEPENDENT MARGINAL COST. Add to each repetition ONE extra atom of type
     (S1) -- one cell, one channel, the shared dispatch channel r0, capacity 1. Everything else
     unchanged. Two experiments, IDENTICAL cell counts:
       - the added atom has NO outgoing precedence arc (nothing waits for it):  T grew by 0.22
       - the added atom is inserted into a chain that feeds a type-(S2) atom:   T grew by 0.49
     Ratio 2.2 at identical occupancy. Linearity was then confirmed in the second regime: adding
     513 such atoms grew T by 16.2 percent against 17.0 predicted by linear extrapolation, and
     766 atoms by 23.8 against 25.4. So the marginal cost is a stable rational number in (0,1),
     not 0 and not 1, and it depends on the atom's position in the precedence relation.

(E2) A STRICT ORDER REVERSAL BETWEEN COUNT AND PERIOD. Two arrangements P and Q of the same
     computation. Counting occupancies of the dispatch channel r0 per repetition: P uses 2814,
     Q uses 3142 (Q is 11.7 percent HEAVIER). Measured periods: Q is 2 percent SHORTER than P.
     So the total count of r0-cells is not even ordinally faithful to T*. My suspicion is that
     the binding constraint sits in a different channel (the transfer channel r2), where P is
     heavier -- but I want the model to make this a theorem, not a guess.

(E3) A CHANNEL DISCOVERED, NOT ASSUMED. Two phases of the same computation carry EXACTLY the
     same number of type-(S2) atoms (256 each) and EXACTLY the same number of type-(S1) atoms
     (208 each), yet their measured durations differ by 795 units. The difference matches, to
     within 3.4 percent, the difference of their loads in the transfer channel r2 (1024 versus
     256 units of r2-time), computed from the shape of the transfer requests. So the binding
     channel was r2, invisible in the r0 count. This is why I need the model to CERTIFY which
     channel binds, rather than take it as input.

(E4) A NON-MONOTONICITY UNDER A SIDE CONSTRAINT. Removing 96 atoms made T LARGER. The cause is a
     side resource I have so far kept outside the model: a bounded pool of Q "slots" (Q = 255 per
     agent, hard) that must hold every value between its creation and its last use; when the pool
     overflows, extra atoms of a costly type are injected automatically. So the atom set itself
     is not fixed -- it is determined BY the placement, through a maximum-weight-live constraint.

============================================================
4. THE TWO QUESTIONS
============================================================

QUESTION 1 (structure => tractability, with certificate).

Give the sharpest structural condition on the family {F_i} under which T* equals a min-max
expression
        T* = max over S in a family Sigma of ( load(S) / capacity(S) )
(i.e. the natural relaxation is exact) and state the dual certificate that proves optimality of a
given placement. I care specifically about which of the following are sufficient, which are
necessary, and which are neither:

   (a) each footprint is a contiguous run of offsets inside a SINGLE channel;
   (b) all footprints touch a common channel at offset 0 and are otherwise disjoint in channels
       (this is the shape (S2)/(S3) above);
   (c) the atom-by-cell incidence matrix is an interval matrix / totally unimodular;
   (d) the admissible subsets form the independent sets of a matroid, or of an intersection of
       two matroids;
   (e) the supports {d : (r,d) in F_i} form a laminar family under inclusion.

Then: where is the exact complexity boundary? What is the SMALLEST departure from your tractable
class that makes the minimum period NP-hard -- two channels instead of one? capacity 2 instead
of 1? footprints of size 2 with a gap (offsets {0,2}) instead of contiguous {0,1}? Please give
the reduction, or cite the shape of it. I want to know exactly how much structure I may lose.

QUESTION 2 (the marginal, and the reversal -- interdependent with Q1).

Explain (E1) inside the model of Q1. My hypothesis: the true optimum is a max over a family Sigma
of subsets, and the marginal derivative of T* with respect to inserting one new cell equals the
"tightness density" -- the fraction of the tight subsets in Sigma that the new cell joins, or
equivalently the value of an optimal dual variable. That would make the marginal a rational in
(0,1) and make it depend on the atom's position in D, because the position decides which subsets
of Sigma it joins.

  (i) Is that the correct formalisation? If yes, give Sigma and the dual certificate explicitly,
      and give a computable expression for the marginal: "adding one cell of channel r to an
      instance whose tight family is ... raises T* by exactly ...". If it is wrong, give the
      correct account of a marginal strictly between 0 and 1 that also explains why the SAME
      added cell costs 2.2x more when it has a successor.
  (ii) Under your answer, is the marginal locally CONSTANT (a linear regime) and over what range?
       (E1)'s 513/766-atom check says yes over at least an 11 percent range; I want the condition
       for that, and the point where the regime breaks.
  (iii) What minimal extra data must be recorded per atom so that the reversal (E2) is PREDICTED
       rather than explained after the fact? I want the smallest sufficient statistic: what must
       I know about an atom besides its cell count in r0.

Optional if it costs you little: say what (E4) does to the theory -- an atom set that depends on
the placement through a bounded "liveness" pool. Is that still in a class with any guarantee, or
does it break the min-max entirely?

I would rather have one theorem with a proof and an honest boundary than a survey. If the honest
answer is "the class you describe is already NP-hard and here is the reduction", that is a
complete answer and I want it stated that way, together with the best approximation guarantee
that survives.
