## 0. Summary of the answers

1. **Your class (b) — one shared unit-capacity dispatch channel touched at offset 0, plus one private contiguous anchored run per atom — is already strongly NP-hard, with no precedence at all.** I give the reduction (Theorem 3). Exactness of the min–max fails even earlier: with just **two** secondary channels there is a parity counterexample with a duality gap (Proposition 4). The tractable/exact cells are precisely: pure precedence; one channel with contiguous runs; dispatch + **one** unit-width secondary channel; and the span-1 matroid case (Theorem 2).
2. The certificate that survives everywhere is not a family of atom-subsets but a family of **circuits in a resource-serialized graph**; volume bounds are degenerate circuits. A given placement is certified optimal *within its serialization class* by a critical circuit with l(C) = T·w(C). Your marginal hypothesis is correct after replacing "tight subsets" by "optimal dual circulation": the marginal is the normalized dual flow the new cell must absorb, minimized over admissible insertion points — rational, in (0,1), position-dependent, locally constant (Theorem 5). This reproduces (E1) quantitatively and (E2)/(E3) structurally.

Throughout, an instance is (V, {F_i}, {m_r}, D, l, w); T* is the minimum feasible period; "PERIOD ≤ T" is the decision problem (in NP: the residues s_i mod T are a poly-size certificate).

## 1. The valid bound family Σ and the dual certificate

**Definition 1 (serialization).** For channel r with capacity m_r and contiguous per-atom occupancies, a *serialization* σ assigns r's users to m_r tracks and cyclically orders each track. It adds arcs u→v (v the track-successor of u) with length l = (span of u's run in r) and a turn count w_uv ≥ 0, w's summing around each track to the number of periods the track's cycle spans. Write G_σ = D ∪ (serialization arcs).

**Theorem 1 (exactness for fixed σ; the certificate).** For contiguous footprints and fixed σ, the minimum period among placements inducing σ is
  T*(σ) = max over directed circuits C of G_σ of l(C)/w(C),
computable in polynomial time (max cycle ratio, Karp/Lawler). A placement of period T is optimal in its class iff G_σ contains a *critical circuit* C with l(C) = T·w(C), and then the integer potentials realizing it are the schedule. Dual (LP) form: T*(σ) = max{ Σ l_a f_a : f ≥ 0 a circulation, Σ w_a f_a = 1 }; vertices of this polytope are normalized circuits.

*Proof sketch.* Feasibility of the difference system {s_v − s_u ≥ l_a − T w_a} is Farkas-equivalent to every circuit having l(C) ≤ T w(C); integrality of T yields integral longest-path potentials. ∎

Both classical bounds are members of this family: (B2) uses only D-arcs; (B1) for channel r is the circuit going once around a track (Σl = track volume, Σw = 1), maximized over the best track split, giving ⌈vol_r/m_r⌉. Windowed ("energetic") bounds — "all of S must dispatch inside a precedence window, but S carries vol_r(S) cells" — are hybrid circuits: precedence paths closing a track segment. So:

**The family Σ = circuits of serialized completions.** T* = min over σ of max over C ∈ G_σ — a genuine min–max with the min *outside*. The order-free relaxation max(B1, B2, energetic bounds) is always a lower bound; the content of Question 1 is when it is exact. Note a structural caveat: serialization theory itself *presupposes contiguity* — gapped footprints can interleave, so the feasible set is not a union of order-polyhedra. Gaps are therefore the hardness frontier twice over.

## 2. The exact/tractable cells

**Theorem 2.** T* equals the order-free min–max, in polynomial time, in each of these cells:

**(i) No resources.** T* = max_C l(C)/w(C) (Theorem 1 with empty σ).

**(ii) One channel, contiguous runs, any capacity m, no precedence.** T* = ⌈Σp_i/m⌉. *Proof:* concatenate the runs on a line of length L = Σp_i and fold x ↦ x mod T with T = ⌈L/m⌉. Each run (p_i ≤ T) maps to a cyclic interval; each residue is covered ⌈L/T⌉ ≤ m times. ∎ (Note: no total unimodularity is used or available — see (c) below.)

**(iii) Dispatch + one secondary channel (unit widths).** Atoms are singletons {(r0,0)} or {(r0,0)} ∪ {(r1, 0..k_i−1)}, m_{r0} = m_{r1} = 1, k_i arbitrary, no precedence. Then T* = max(n, vol_{r1}). *Proof:* pack the r1-runs consecutively from 0; their starts are the partial sums, distinct residues inside [0, vol_{r1}); singletons fill any of the T − h ≥ n − h remaining residues. ∎ (Extends to m_{r1} ≥ 2 by staggering the folded laps; to m_{r0} ≥ 2 trivially.)

**(iv) Span-1 matroid case.** If every footprint is {(·,0)} and "placeable at one instant" is independence in one matroid M, then (no precedence) T* = max_{∅≠A⊆V} ⌈|A|/r_M(A)⌉ — Edmonds' covering theorem, poly via matroid union, dual certificate the maximizing set A.

These four cells are the whole tractable/exact region I can defend, and each fails under the smallest perturbation, as follows.

## 3. The hardness boundary

**Theorem 3 (your class is hard).** PERIOD ≤ T is strongly NP-complete for instances of shape (b): every footprint is {(r0,0)} ∪ (one contiguous run at offsets 0..k−1 in one private channel), all capacities 1, **no precedence**.

*Reduction from 3-Partition* (a_1..a_{3q}, B/4 < a_j < B/2, Σa_j = qB). Set T = q(B+4). Channels: r0; a wall channel W; item channels C_1..C_{3q}. Atoms: q wall atoms {(r0,0)} ∪ {(W,0..B+3)}; for each j one *body* atom {(r0,0)} ∪ {(C_j, 0..T−a_j−1)} and a_j *unit* atoms {(r0,0),(C_j,0)}. Every channel has volume exactly T, and n = q + Σ(a_j+1) = T, so at period T every channel must be *exactly tiled* and dispatch is a bijection. Tiling W by q equal intervals forces the wall dispatch cells to be an arithmetic progression of step B+4, cutting Z_T into q chambers of B+3 consecutive free dispatch residues. Tiling C_j forces its body run to occupy [x_j, x_j+T−a_j) and its units the remaining a_j cells individually — so C_j's dispatch cells are a_j+1 **consecutive** residues. These 3q blocks partition the chambers; the size window B/4+1 < a_j+1 < B/2+1 forces exactly three blocks per chamber, summing to B+3, i.e. Σa = B per chamber. Conversely a 3-partition yields the placement. ∎

**Corollary 3′ (single-channel form).** One channel, capacity 1, no precedence, all footprints contiguous intervals **except one atom whose footprint is an arithmetic progression with equal gaps**: strongly NP-complete. (Take the wall as that one atom, items as intervals of length a_j+1.) So the smallest departure from contiguity that kills tractability is *one* non-contiguous footprint.

**Proposition 4 (exactness dies at two secondary channels).** Dispatch + channels A, B: A has 2 atoms with runs of length 3, B has 3 atoms with runs of length 2, all capacities 1, no precedence. Bounds: n = 5, vol_A = vol_B = 6, so LB = 6. At T = 6 both channels must tile: A's starts are {x, x+3} (opposite parities), B's starts a full parity class — the dispatch cells collide, so T* = 7 (a valid 7-schedule: B at {0,2,5}… A at {1,4}… wait — explicitly B starts {0,2,4}, A starts {1,5}; all distinct, runs disjoint). Gap = 1 with all footprints contiguous and anchored. ∎

**Other minimal departures.**
- **Precedence + any shared channel:** strongly NP-hard even in cell (iii). Embed sequencing-within-intervals (1|r_j,d_j| feasibility, strongly NP-hard): an anchor atom z, arcs z→j (l = r_j, w = 0) and j→z (l = T₀ − d_j + p_j, w = 1) create the windows [r_j, d_j − p_j] relative to z at T = T₀; runs p_j live on one channel. With *unit* runs and windows only, it is bipartite matching (poly) — so hardness needs variable run lengths, or unit runs with general latency DAGs (Hennessy–Gross).
- **Identical gapped footprint for all atoms** (your "{0,2} instead of {0,1}"): *not* the frontier. For any fixed shape, min T is decided by a finite collision-vector automaton (Davidson's pipeline theory), poly for bounded span; but exactness of B1 fails by parity: n atoms of shape {0,2}, capacity 1, have T* = 2n for n even and 2n+1 for n odd. Hardness needs **unboundedly many distinct shapes** (variable gaps), per Corollary 3′.
- **Capacity 2 instead of 1:** harmless — folding handles any m; the reductions also survive m ≥ 2 by duplication. Capacity is not the boundary.
- **Widths (S3):** kill exactness even in cell (iii). Atoms (c,k) = (128,1) and (64,2) in a width-128 channel: volume bound 2, but they cannot share an instant, T* = 3. Cyclic strip packing; volume is off by up to a factor ≈ 2, dual-feasible-function bounds are the right certificates.

**Verdicts on (a)–(e).** None is necessary (the four tractable cells don't satisfy any of them uniformly). (a) sufficient only without precedence (Thm 2ii; windows reduction kills it with precedence). (b) insufficient even without precedence (Thm 3; Prop 4); sufficient with ≤ 1 secondary channel (Thm 2iii). (c) neither: cyclic wrap gives circular-ones, not TU, and the LP is fractionally feasible at T = 6 in Prop 4 while T* = 7; the exact cells are exact by *folding*, not integrality. (d) one matroid, span 1: sufficient (Edmonds); two matroids: covering by common independent sets is already NP-hard (edge coloring, Holyer); temporal extent ≥ 2 leaves the framework. (e) laminar/dyadic supports: insufficient — the width counterexample above is dyadic, and dyadic-aligned runs reproduce Prop 4's parity clash; dyadicity helps only as a packing discipline (buddy alignment removes fragmentation *within one channel*), never restores the min–max.

**The boundary, stated once:** exactness of the order-free min–max is lost as soon as two independently saturable rigid structures must share the dispatch residues (two secondary channels, or widths); NP-hardness arrives with unboundedly many distinct rigid shapes (variable run lengths across ≥ Θ(q) channels, or one AP footprint, or variable gaps), or with precedence windows alongside any shared channel. The open cell: dispatch + exactly two secondary channels, bounded k, no precedence — non-exact (Prop 4) but its complexity I will not claim.

## 4. Question 2: the marginal, the linear regime, the reversal

**Theorem 5 (marginal = normalized dual flow).** Fix an optimal placement with serialization σ* and let f* be an optimal dual circulation of Theorem 1 (if the critical circuit C* is unique, f* = 1_{C*}/w(C*)). Insert one new atom x; an *insertion site* is a splice into each track of each channel x uses, upstream of all its successors and downstream of its predecessors; splicing x between u,v replaces arc u→v (length p_u) by u→x→v, adding length p_x, and adds x's latency arcs. Then the right derivative of T* under λ copies of x per period is
  dT*/dλ = min over admissible insertion sites π of Σ_{a ∈ π} f*_a · δ_a(x),
where δ_a is the length added on arc a. With a unique critical circuit this is (occupancy + latency the insertion forces onto C*)/w(C*).

So: **(i)** Your formalisation is correct after one amendment: Σ is not a family of atom-subsets but of *circuits* (equivalently, the dual variable is a circulation; energetic/windowed subsets are the hybrid circuits). "Tightness density" = the dual flow through the cells the new atom must absorb. This is exactly why the marginal is a rational in (0,1): denominators are turn counts w(C*) (or convex combinations over tied circuits when the dual is degenerate), and it is 1 only if the binding circuit has w = 1 (e.g. a saturated volume bound), 0 only if a zero-flow splice exists. And it is position-dependent for precisely the reason you conjectured with the corrected Σ: a successor **shrinks the set of admissible insertion sites** — x must be spliced upstream of its consumer, where the critical circulation is denser, and its latency arc itself may join C*. That is (E1): 0.22 with a free splice, 0.49 = 2.2× when forced upstream of an (S2) atom, identical cell counts. The measured values estimate (δl, w) of the critical circulation; e.g. 0.22 ≈ 2/9, 0.49 ≈ 4/9 is one consistent reading — the theory's falsifiable prediction is that all probe marginals are ratios with a common small denominator structure.

**(ii) Local constancy.** T*(λ) = min over σ of max over C of a family of affine functions of λ, hence piecewise linear. The slope is constant while (σ*, argmax circuit) is stable: condition = strict gap between the critical ratio and the runner-up ρ₂, giving a linear range of ≈ w·(T* − ρ₂)/δl inserted atoms; the breakpoint is where ρ₂ catches up or a re-serialization becomes profitable. The outer min makes T*(λ) sub-additive under re-optimization — *concave* deviations from the linear extrapolation — which is exactly your data: 16.2 measured vs 17.0 extrapolated, 23.8 vs 25.4. An 11% linear regime means the critical circuit's ratio dominates its runner-up by that margin.

**(iii) Sufficient statistic for (E2).** Per atom record: the **per-channel weighted volume vector** vol_r(i) (for width channels, the area c_i·k_i), the **shape pair (c_i, k_i)** for width channels (fragmentation/DFF corrections — area alone misses the 128-wide serialization effect), and, for circuit terms, the latency/turn position (longest-path distances into and out of the recurrence circuits). The r0 count alone is *ordinally meaningless* — (E2) is the witness. With the vector recorded, the reversal is a theorem, certified two-sidedly: exhibit a feasible placement of Q at T_Q (primal) and the r2-volume circuit of P proving T*(P) ≥ ⌈vol_{r2}(P)/128⌉ > T_Q (dual). (E3) is the same certificate: 795 ≈ Δvol_{r2}/m_{r2} to 3.4% is the r2 volume bound binding in both phases — "which channel binds" = which channel carries the critical circulation, and the certificate *names* it.

## 5. (E4): placement-dependent atom sets

A liveness pool is one more channel whose footprints have *decision-dependent* length: value v occupies "slot channel" for s_use(v) − s_def(v) + const + T·w_v instants, and Σ_v lifetime ≤ Q·T. This is still **linear** in (s, T), so it joins the LP family — the min–max lower bound extends with "register circuits" whose denominators involve Q (this is the MaxLive constraint of modulo scheduling). What breaks the theory is not the pool but the **recourse**: automatic injection of spill atoms makes the instance a function of the placement — a bilevel problem. Monotonicity fails exactly as in (E4): removing atoms stretches lifetimes past QT, injects r2-heavy spills, and raises the binding r2 volume. No min–max survives the recourse; what survives is the valid LP lower bound with spill variables (a MILP), and monotonicity is restored only if worst-case spill volume is priced into Σ a priori.

## 6. What survives as a guarantee

For contiguous shapes with precedence: decomposed software pipelining (retime by a B2-optimal potential, then fold-pack the acyclic remainder) gives T ≤ B2 + O(1)·B1 — constant factor ≤ 2 + o(1) (Gasperoni–Schwiegelshohn; Calland–Darte–Robert). For the width channel alone: cyclic strip packing admits (1+ε)·B1 + O(k_max) asymptotically (Kenyon–Rémila, folded to the cylinder), with DFFs as the matching certificates. Beyond constant factor, approximability of the joint problem is open; nothing rules out a PTAS, but no known technique handles the residue-coupling of Theorem 3's gadgets.

**One-line honest answer:** your class is NP-hard and the order-free min–max has a gap of at least parity type, but the *serialized-circuit* min–max is exact per class with a polynomial critical-circuit certificate, its dual circulation is the marginal you measured, and the per-atom (area, shape, circuit-position) vector is the smallest statistic that turns (E1)–(E3) from observations into theorems.
